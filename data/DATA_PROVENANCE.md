# Data Provenance

This document traces every input file in `data/` back to its source. Every value used by the framework code in `multicrisis/` and `scripts/` is reproducible from these inputs. No values were generated by hand; no values were estimated to fill gaps. Numbers that are missing in the raw source are missing in the downstream files too.

## File-by-file documentation

### `raw/dataset_25.09.25.xlsx` and `raw/dataset_25.09.25.csv`

The annual water balance for Israel, 1959–2023, as published by the **Israel Water Authority** (Hebrew: Reshut HaMayim). Columns include total water produced by source (wells, Kinneret, upper-water, effluents reuse, desalination), water consumed by sector (agricultural, domestic, industrial, environmental), price tariffs, climate variables (temperature, precipitation, drought index), energy consumption, and conflict/cyber scenario flags. Both the original .xlsx and an unmodified CSV export are provided; they contain identical numerical content.

**Source URL:** [https://www.gov.il/en/departments/water_authority](https://www.gov.il/en/departments/water_authority) (master data; specific snapshot dated 2025-09-25).

**Note on year coverage:** the framework discards rows with year < 1990 during processing because earlier years have substantial missing-data gaps in several supply columns. Coverage 1990–2023 is complete.

### `raw/validated_baseline_master.csv`

Cross-checked version of the raw IWA data after a row-by-row validation pass against published Israel Water Authority annual reports for the years 1990–2023. Used as the consistency-check oracle during baseline processing. Any divergence between this file and the raw .xlsx for the 1990–2023 window is documented in `intermediate/baseline_methodology.md`.

### `intermediate/baseline_calibration_parameters.csv`

Sustainable yield and reuse caps used by the forward projection from 2024 to 2050. Two rows:
- `SustainableGroundwaterCap = 500 MCM/yr` — estimated sustainable annual groundwater abstraction
- `WastewaterReuseCap = 800 MCM/yr` — projected upper bound on usable treated effluent by 2050

**Source:** Tal, A. (2018) "Addressing Desalination's Carbon Footprint: The Israeli Experience", *Water* 10, 197 (Ref 32 in main text); Israel Water Authority Master Plan 2050 (Ref 37 in main text).

### `intermediate/baseline_expansion_schedule.csv` and `intermediate/baseline_expansions.csv`

Year-by-year desalination capacity additions used by the forward projection. Reflect the published Israel Water Authority desalination infrastructure pipeline (Sorek 2 commissioning, Western Galilee, etc.).

**Source:** Israel Water Authority Master Plan 2050 (Ref 37 in main text); cross-checked against operator websites (Sorek, IDE Technologies).

### `intermediate/adaptation_triggers.csv`

Rules-based adaptation logic for the forward projection: thresholds at which the baseline trajectory increments desalination capacity or reuse infrastructure in response to projected demand growth or supply shortfall. Used only during baseline projection, not during the crisis-scenario analysis.

### `intermediate/baseline_historical_data_1990_2023.csv`

The 1990–2023 subset of the raw IWA data after column harmonisation (renaming inconsistent header strings to a single schema). All numerical values are unchanged from the raw source; only column names differ.

### `intermediate/baseline_forward_projections_2025_2050.csv`

The 2025–2050 forward projection produced by the baseline-processing notebook. Demand grows at population-adjusted rates; desalination expansion follows `baseline_expansion_schedule.csv`; groundwater and surface-water projections follow the historical trend with a stationarity assumption. Documented in `baseline_methodology.md`.

### `intermediate/baseline_trend_analysis_results.csv` and `intermediate/baseline_variability_analysis.csv`

Descriptive statistics computed during baseline processing: per-column linear trends, breakpoint analyses, and interannual variability metrics for the historical record. Not directly consumed by the framework — provided here for transparency about how the forward projection was derived.

### `intermediate/baseline_methodology.md`

Narrative explanation of the baseline-processing steps. Read this for the audit trail of how `raw/dataset_25.09.25.xlsx` becomes `baseline_data_processed.csv`.

### `intermediate/baseline_summary.json`

Compact JSON summary of key baseline statistics (mean supply by source, mean demand by sector, projected 2050 values). Cross-check against the manuscript's Section 2.5 narrative.

### `baseline_data_processed.csv`

The **single file the multicrisis framework code consumes**. Sixty-one rows (1990–2050), 104 columns. Produced from the raw + intermediate files above by the original baseline-processing notebook (`../notebooks/baseline_processing_ORIGINAL.ipynb`).

Required columns consumed by `multicrisis/crises.py`:
- `year`
- `water_desalination_mcm`
- `capped_groundwater`
- `produced_by_kinneret_milion_m3`
- `capped_reuse`
- `total_produced_by_upper_water_milion_m3`
- `total_demand`
- (sector demand columns optional, used when conflict-induced demand changes propagate)

### `drought_params.csv`, `energy_params.csv`, `conflict_params.csv`, `cyber_params.csv`

Per-severity crisis parameters. Each file has columns `Parameter, Optimistic, Moderate, Pessimistic` plus a `Notes` or duration column. Values are tabulated and justified in **Supplementary Table S3** of the manuscript. Sources:

- **drought** — Spinoni et al. 2018 (Ref 36), Cook et al. 2018 (Ref 3)
- **energy** — Busby et al. 2021 (Ref 6), Bazilian et al. 2011 (Ref 35)
- **conflict** — Gleick 2014 (Ref 4), Gleick 2019
- **cyber** — Hassanzadeh et al. 2020 (Ref 7), Shapira et al. 2021 (Ref 34)

### `compound_crisis_impact_summary.csv`

Archived Tier 1 (additive-only) reference output produced by the original baseline-processing notebook. Used solely by `scripts/task0_sanity_check.py` as the regression oracle confirming that the clean module extraction in `multicrisis/` produces bit-exact identical results for the additive-only case (max diff 1.42e-14, machine epsilon).

This file is *not* used by Task 1–6 — it is a reproducibility test artefact only.

## Data availability statement for the manuscript

The complete reproducibility chain — raw Israel Water Authority data, intermediate processing files, crisis parameter tables, baseline processing notebook, and the analytical Python framework — is provided in the supplementary package archived alongside this manuscript. Raw water-balance data are also publicly available from the Israel Water Authority (https://www.gov.il/en/departments/water_authority). All numerical results reported in the main text and supplementary materials are reproducible by following the instructions in `../README.md`.

## What is NOT in this package

- **Spatial or daily-resolution data**: the framework operates on the published annual aggregated balance only. Sub-annual and spatially disaggregated water-system data for Israel are held by the Israel Water Authority and are not part of the published baseline.
- **Climate model outputs**: the forward baseline uses statistical trend extrapolation, not downscaled climate model data. The drought severity parameters reflect Mediterranean drought ranges from Spinoni et al. 2018 rather than scenario-specific CMIP outputs.
- **Cost data**: tariff and OPEX-multiplier columns appear in the raw dataset but are not used by the published headline analysis (which focuses on supply, demand, WSI, and gap metrics).
